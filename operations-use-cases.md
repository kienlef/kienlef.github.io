# Twenty-seven operational decisions agents should not own alone.

<p class="kf-page-lead">This library began with sixteen LinkedIn use cases and now covers twenty-seven operational decisions across the ASCM SCOR Digital Standard. Every case starts with the decision, the people who live with its consequences, and the boundary an agent must respect.</p>

<p class="kf-page-actions"><a class="button button--primary button--pill" href="/operations-intelligence-map/">Explore the interactive relationship map</a></p>

<div class="kf-note">
  <strong>The common thread:</strong> agents can monitor, compare, prepare, classify, predict, optimise, coordinate, and simulate. A named human still owns the trade-off, the exception, and the commitment.
</div>

<section class="kf-section dark">
  <span class="kf-eyebrow"><i class="ph ph-chart-line-up"></i> 01 · Orchestrate & Plan</span>
  <h2>A better plan makes the trade-offs visible.</h2>
  <p>Agents can reconcile signals, simulate scenarios, and surface conflicts. They cannot turn a forecast, segment, or scenario into an organisational commitment by themselves.</p>
</section>

<div class="kf-grid-2">
  <div class="kf-card" id="sop"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>S&OP agents prepare the trade-off, they do not settle it</h3><p>Agents can assemble the scenario pack, reconcile demand and supply views, and expose the gap before the meeting. The consensus number is still a management commitment.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> S&OP / IBP leadership with supply, demand, and production planning</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may model and reconcile. It may not publish a consensus plan without the S&OP owners.</p><p><a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-18-sop">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="segmentation"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Segmentation shows agents where not to generalise</h3><p>Products and customers need different policies. The segmentation has to reflect operating reality, not a tidy clustering exercise.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Supply chain, commercial, and customer-service leaders</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may cluster and propose. Segment policy and service promises stay with commercial and operations owners.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GAHNqEaXFS4">Watch the lecture</a> · <a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-17-segmentation">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="risk"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Risk agents need escalation rules</h3><p>A long risk list is not management. Owners need thresholds, response options, and a clear point where the agent stops.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Risk, procurement, and supply chain leaders</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may watch and rank exposure. Mitigation spend and supplier consequences need a human mandate.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dPRwOyh4b0Y">Watch the lecture</a> · <a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-19-risk-analysis">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="network-design"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Network-design agents simulate options, not commitments</h3><p>Models can compare capacity, cost, resilience, service, and emissions. Leaders still own the irreversible choices.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Network strategy and supply chain leaders</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may run the scenario grid. Footprint decisions are capital decisions and stay with leadership.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h6FMkkTPPnw">Watch the lecture</a> · <a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-22-network-design">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="control-towers"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Control towers do not remove trade-off owners</h3><p>Visibility helps teams see conflicts earlier. It does not decide which customer, site, or product absorbs the impact.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Cross-functional supply chain leaders</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may surface the conflict and the options. Allocation between customers stays a named human call.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GlbDjtRqZ1Y">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="forecasting"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Forecast agents need decision discipline</h3><p>A forecast becomes useful only after a team agrees how bias, overrides, promotions, and uncertainty affect the plan.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Demand planners and commercial partners</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may generate and challenge the baseline. Overrides need a named owner and a recorded reason.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7jM9wyFaZJw">Watch the lecture</a> · <a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-13-demand-forecasting">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="inventory"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Inventory agents make trade-offs visible</h3><p>Stock is a buffer against uncertainty and a cost. Humans still choose which risk the business accepts.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Inventory and supply planners</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may recommend targets. Accepting a service-level risk is a business decision.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pW9szoHlmBo">Watch the lecture</a> · <a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-14-inventory-optimization">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="multi-echelon"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Local inventory agents can create global mess</h3><p>Each node can optimise its own target and still make the network worse. Shared objectives and escalation rules matter.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> End-to-end network planners</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> Node-level autonomy needs a network-level objective, or the agents will compete with each other.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YARQSNma7wQ">Watch the lecture</a> · <a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-15-multi-echelon">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="vmi"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>VMI agents move work, not accountability</h3><p>Handing replenishment to a supplier agent changes who acts first. It does not change who answers for a stockout on the line.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Procurement and supply planning with the supplier</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may replenish inside an agreed band. Band changes are a joint commercial decision.</p><p><a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-16-vmi">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
</div>

<section class="kf-section soft">
  <span class="kf-eyebrow"><i class="ph ph-handshake"></i> 02 · Source & assure</span>
  <h2>Supplier automation still runs on definitions, evidence, and trust.</h2>
  <p style="color:#4B5068;max-width:850px;">Procurement and quality teams need clear data rights, scorecard rules, release authority, and escalation paths before an agent acts across company boundaries.</p>
</section>

<div class="kf-grid-2">
  <div class="kf-card" id="supplier-boundaries"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Supplier agents need trust boundaries</h3><p>Data sharing and commitments do not become neutral because software moves them. Supplier-facing owners define what the agent may see and do.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Procurement and supplier-management owners</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> Agent read and write rights across the company boundary are contractual, not technical, decisions.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TqwE4rvyrH8">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="supplier-performance"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Supplier scorecard agents need an agreed definition of late</h3><p>Automated scorecards fail on definitions long before they fail on data. Confirmed date or requested date changes the whole picture.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Procurement and supplier quality</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may score against an agreed rule set. Escalation and supplier status changes are human.</p><p><a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-01-supplier-performance">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="supplier-capacity"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Capacity signals from suppliers are claims, not facts</h3><p>An agent can track declared capacity, lead-time drift, and allocation behaviour. Whether to believe it, buffer against it, or dual-source is a judgement call.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Procurement and supply planning</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may model exposure. Dual-sourcing and pre-buy commitments need procurement authority.</p><p><a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-03-supplier-capacity">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="raw-materials-quality"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Incoming-quality agents work inside a validated system</h3><p>Trend detection on incoming lots is useful. In a regulated environment the release decision follows a validated procedure, not a model score.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Quality, supplier quality, and procurement</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may flag drift and prioritise inspection. Lot release stays with qualified quality personnel.</p><p><a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-05-raw-materials-quality">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="compliance"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Compliance agents should chase documents, not hide exceptions</h3><p>Agents can follow up missing evidence. Functional owners decide what happens when documents, approvals, and reality do not match.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Quality and compliance owners</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> Chasing is delegable. Deciding that an exception is acceptable is not.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K6SOjemhXP8">Watch the lecture</a> · <a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-04-compliance-tracking">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
</div>

<section class="kf-section dark">
  <span class="kf-eyebrow"><i class="ph ph-factory"></i> 03 · Transform</span>
  <h2>Factory physics gets the first and last word.</h2>
  <p>Models help only when maintenance, quality, planning, engineering, and production teams can challenge the signal and act inside the real operating constraints.</p>
</section>

<div class="kf-grid-2">
  <div class="kf-card" id="predictive-maintenance"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Predictive maintenance is a decision problem first</h3><p>A model can flag risk. People decide when to stop equipment, what to inspect, and which production commitment can move.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Maintenance and engineering leaders</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may predict and prioritise. Stopping a qualified line is a production and quality decision.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=88Do5NlNFTU">Watch the lecture</a> · <a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-09-predictive-maintenance">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="computer-vision"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Computer vision in quality is not AI magic</h3><p>The difficult work is defining defects, evidence, and escalation. The camera is only one part of the quality system.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Quality owners and frontline inspectors</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may detect and classify. Defect definitions, validation, and release stay with quality.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zl92TkCkFEw">Watch the lecture</a> · <a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-10-ai-quality">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="production-scheduling"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Scheduling agents cannot remove factory physics</h3><p>Agents can compare schedules quickly. Planners still own feasibility, line constraints, changeovers, and the cost of nervous replanning.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Production planners and shop-floor leads</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may propose a sequence. Freezing and releasing the schedule stays with production.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q7-MhGCMikw">Watch the lecture</a> · <a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-07-production-scheduling">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="batch-size"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Batch-size agents cannot ignore changeover pain</h3><p>Mathematical efficiency can collide with cleaning, setup, expiry, campaign, and service constraints.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Production and planning leaders</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may optimise lot sizes. Campaign structure and cleaning validation set the hard limits.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RhOjwnCaALo">Watch the lecture</a> · <a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-08-batch-size">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="oee"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>OEE agents can only measure what the floor honestly records</h3><p>Automated OEE exposes losses fast. It also exposes how loss reasons are coded, which is a people problem before it is an analytics problem.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Production leadership and manufacturing engineering</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may compute and rank losses. Reason codes and improvement priorities need the line teams.</p><p><a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-12-oee">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="manufacturing-cost"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Cost agents need one version of the cost model</h3><p>Product cost analysis is only as good as routings, BOMs, and overhead logic. Agents amplify a shared cost model or amplify the argument about it.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Manufacturing engineering, production, and controlling</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may analyse variance and simulate. Standard-cost changes stay with controlling.</p><p><a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-06-manufacturing-cost">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="digital-twins"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Digital twins need boring process truth</h3><p>Simulation helps only when states, assumptions, and validation stay close to the real process.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Engineering and operations owners</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> A twin may advise. It may not become the record of what actually happened.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h5GHI3gjuJo">Watch the lecture</a> · <a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-11-digital-twin">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
</div>

<section class="kf-section soft">
  <span class="kf-eyebrow"><i class="ph ph-truck"></i> 04 · Order, Fulfill & Return</span>
  <h2>Customer promises and reverse flows expose the real decision boundary.</h2>
  <p style="color:#4B5068;max-width:850px;">Prediction and optimisation are useful. Spending money, changing a promise, approving a credit, or deciding disposition still needs explicit human authority.</p>
</section>

<div class="kf-grid-2">
  <div class="kf-card" id="customer-satisfaction"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Satisfaction agents read signals, people repair trust</h3><p>Text, tickets, and delivery data can be mined for early dissatisfaction. The recovery gesture, and its cost, is a human choice.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Customer service and commercial operations leaders</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may detect and summarise sentiment. It may not make goodwill commitments to a customer.</p><p><a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-20-customer-satisfaction">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="fulfillment"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Fulfillment agents must protect customer trust</h3><p>Shortages and delays become customer-facing decisions. People decide which promises can flex and which ones protect trust.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Fulfillment and customer-service leaders</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may re-sequence and propose. Breaking a promised date is a commercial conversation.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RyU0Y_Ioy5s">Watch the lecture</a> · <a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-21-order-fulfillment">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="delivery-time"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>A better ETA is only useful if someone acts on it</h3><p>Predicting a late delivery early is cheap. Deciding to expedite, re-route, or call the customer is the part that costs something.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Transport, dispatch, and customer-service leads</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may predict and alert. Expediting spend and customer notification follow agreed thresholds.</p><p><a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-24-delivery-time">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="routing"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Routing agents need human exception logic</h3><p>A neat route can ignore a driver, loading dock, safety issue, or customer promise. Dispatch defines the exceptions that matter.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Dispatch and logistics leads</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may optimise the plan. Driver, safety, and site exceptions override the optimum.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=My7X1fSJL7k">Watch the lecture</a> · <a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-25-route-optimization">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="transportation-cost"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Freight-cost agents expose the drivers, not the deal</h3><p>Lane-level analysis shows where cost, mode, and service diverge. Carrier strategy and negotiation stay with people who own the relationship.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Transport management and procurement</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may analyse and prepare the negotiation fact base. It does not commit volume to a carrier.</p><p><a href="https://kienlef.github.io/operations_use_case_selection/index.html#case-23-transportation-cost">Read full analytical case</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="returns"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-arrow-elbow-down-right"></i></div><h3>Return agents can triage, but disposition is a judgement</h3><p>Reverse flows carry condition, entitlement, and compliance questions at once. An agent can classify and route the case; scrap, rework, or credit remains a decision with cost and quality consequences.</p><p><strong>Decision owner:</strong> Customer service, quality, and warehouse leaders</p><p><strong>Agent boundary:</strong> The agent may triage and prepare. Disposition, credit, and scrap approvals stay with quality and commercial owners.</p><p><a href="/operations-intelligence-map/">Explore this case in the map</a></p></div>
</div>
