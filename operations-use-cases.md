# Sixteen operational decisions agents should not own alone.

<p class="kf-page-lead">I wrote this series because operational AI becomes vague very quickly. These sixteen cases start with a concrete decision, the people who live with its consequences, and the boundary an agent must respect. The sequence moves from the factory, through planning and network choices, to supplier and customer commitments.</p>

<div class="kf-note">
  <strong>The common thread:</strong> agents can monitor, compare, prepare, chase, and simulate. A named human still owns the trade-off, the exception, and the commitment.
</div>

<section class="kf-section dark">
  <span class="kf-eyebrow"><i class="ph ph-factory"></i> 01 · Make</span>
  <h2>Factory physics gets the first and last word.</h2>
  <p>Models help only when maintenance, quality, planning, and production teams can challenge the signal and act on it.</p>
</section>

<div class="kf-grid-2">
  <div class="kf-card" id="predictive-maintenance"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-wrench"></i></div><h3>Predictive maintenance is a decision problem first</h3><p>An agent can detect a pattern. Maintenance and production still decide whether to stop, inspect, defer, or accept the risk.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=88Do5NlNFTU">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="computer-vision"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-eye"></i></div><h3>Computer vision in quality is not AI magic</h3><p>False positives and false negatives land in a quality system, not a demo. Quality owners define validation and escalation.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zl92TkCkFEw">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="production-scheduling"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-calendar-check"></i></div><h3>Scheduling agents cannot remove factory physics</h3><p>Setup times, skills, material, release status, and the surprise at 2 p.m. remain real. Production planners own the compromise.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q7-MhGCMikw">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="digital-twins"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-cube-focus"></i></div><h3>Digital twins need boring process truth</h3><p>A useful twin is honest enough to disappoint you. People closest to execution know where the model stops matching the process.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h5GHI3gjuJo">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="batch-size"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-stack"></i></div><h3>Batch-size agents cannot ignore changeover pain</h3><p>Clean mathematics collides with cleaning, changeovers, labour, shelf life, and service promises. Team leads shape the rules.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RhOjwnCaALo">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
</div>

<section class="kf-section soft">
  <span class="kf-eyebrow"><i class="ph ph-chart-line-up"></i> 02 · Plan</span>
  <h2>Better visibility creates sharper trade-offs, not automatic answers.</h2>
  <p style="color:#4B5068;max-width:850px;">Planning agents can compare scenarios and keep signals moving. They cannot decide which shortage, buffer, customer promise, or network commitment the organisation should accept.</p>
</section>

<div class="kf-grid-2">
  <div class="kf-card" id="control-towers"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-monitor"></i></div><h3>Control towers do not remove trade-off owners</h3><p>The dashboard is the easy part. Operations leaders decide what happens when service, inventory, capacity, and cost point in different directions.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GlbDjtRqZ1Y">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="forecasting"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-trend-up"></i></div><h3>Forecast agents need decision discipline</h3><p>The forecast is not the decision. Planners translate error into capacity, inventory, customer commitments, and escalation.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7jM9wyFaZJw">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="inventory"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-package"></i></div><h3>Inventory agents make trade-offs visible</h3><p>Too much stock hurts. Too little stock hurts louder. Decision owners balance service, cash, shelf life, capacity, and trust.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pW9szoHlmBo">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="multi-echelon"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-tree-structure"></i></div><h3>Local inventory agents can create global mess</h3><p>A warehouse can look efficient while the network gets worse. Network-level owners protect the system from one-site optimisation.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YARQSNma7wQ">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="segmentation"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-selection"></i></div><h3>Segmentation shows agents where not to generalise</h3><p>Products, customers, suppliers, and routes are not interchangeable. Commercial and operations leaders define meaningful exceptions.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GAHNqEaXFS4">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="risk"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-warning-diamond"></i></div><h3>Risk agents need escalation rules</h3><p>A warning without an agreed response is another alert. Risk owners define thresholds, priorities, and who acts next.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dPRwOyh4b0Y">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="network-design"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-map-trifold"></i></div><h3>Network-design agents simulate options, not commitments</h3><p>Models can compare footprints, suppliers, buffers, and lead times. Leaders must live with the commitments after the model closes.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h6FMkkTPPnw">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
</div>

<section class="kf-section dark">
  <span class="kf-eyebrow"><i class="ph ph-handshake"></i> 03 · Cross boundaries</span>
  <h2>Trust becomes part of the operating system.</h2>
  <p>Supplier data, compliance evidence, dispatch exceptions, and customer promises cross organisational boundaries. Automation needs explicit rights and limits.</p>
</section>

<div class="kf-grid-2">
  <div class="kf-card" id="supplier-boundaries"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-handshake"></i></div><h3>Supplier agents need trust boundaries</h3><p>Data sharing and commitments do not become neutral because software moves them. Supplier-facing owners define what the agent may see and do.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TqwE4rvyrH8">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="compliance"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-file-search"></i></div><h3>Compliance agents should chase documents, not hide exceptions</h3><p>Agents can follow up missing evidence. Functional owners decide what happens when documents, approvals, and reality do not match.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K6SOjemhXP8">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="routing"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-path"></i></div><h3>Routing agents need human exception logic</h3><p>A neat route can ignore a driver, loading dock, safety issue, or customer promise. Dispatch leads define the exceptions that matter.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=My7X1fSJL7k">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
  <div class="kf-card" id="fulfillment"><div class="kf-icon"><i class="ph ph-package"></i></div><h3>Fulfillment agents must protect customer trust</h3><p>Shortages and delays become customer-facing decisions. Fulfillment leaders decide which promises can flex and which ones protect trust.</p><p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RyU0Y_Ioy5s">Watch the lecture</a></p></div>
</div>
